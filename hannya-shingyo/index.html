More about the Hannya Shingyō

What is the Hannya Shingyō?

The Hannya Shingyō (摩訶般若波羅蜜多心経, Maka Hannya Haramita Shingyō, sometimes written Hannya Shingyo), or Heart Sutra, is one of the shortest and most widely recited texts of Mahāyāna Buddhism. Its Chinese version, attributed to the monk Xuanzang (7th century), runs to barely 260 characters1. It distils the teaching of the “perfection of wisdom” (prajñāpāramitā): nothing exists by itself, “form is emptiness, emptiness is form”.

Why chant it?

In the Sōtō school, the Heart Sutra is chanted every day: at morning service in temples, and often after zazen in dojos2. In many dojos, especially in Europe, it is chanted not in translation but in its Japanese reading of the Chinese characters. Knowing it by heart lets you chant with the others without keeping your eyes on the sutra book.

How is it chanted?

The chant follows the mokugyo, the fish-shaped wooden drum: one beat per syllable, in a low, steady voice, without melody. The kokyō (the ino in Japan) announces the title alone, then the whole assembly joins in. The bells, keisu and shokei, mark the beginning and the end. The tempo and some details vary from one dojo and lineage to another: follow what is done in yours.

Learning to chant the Hannya Shingyō on this site

  1. Listen: the recording highlights each kanji as it is chanted, with the kana and the rōmaji.
  2. Chant with the bells: keisu, mokugyo and shokei sound where they belong, as in the dojo.
  3. Chant with the mic: speech recognition, which works offline, checks your recitation kanji by kanji.
  4. Review: each day, the lines to go over, until you can chant from memory in dojo mode, one line at a time.

The tool is free and needs no sign-up. It does not replace a dojo or a teacher: it helps you memorise the text so that you can chant with the sangha.
